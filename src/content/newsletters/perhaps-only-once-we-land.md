This will be a short missive because I am once again sitting in the Air Canada Maple Leaf Lounge at SFO waiting for boarding. This time I’m en route to New Orleans, possibly the last must-see American city[^city] I haven’t been to yet. So! Quite excited.

One new thing I’m trying on this trip, which I probably picked up from Tyler Cowen back when I still read [Marginal Revolution](https://marginalrevolution.com/)[^cowen], is reading a history of the city before I arrive. I ended up landing on [_New Orleans: A Concise History of an Exceptional City_](https://lsupress.org/9780807184875/new-orleans/), a recent book by Charles D. Chamberlain, which is exactly what it says on the tin. I’m learning a lot of basic facts — the ongoing legacy of French Catholicism and the (_relatively_ lax, compared to Anglo-America) Code Noir slavery codes, an explanation for the (in)famous above-ground cemeteries, an overview of its explosive population growth[^growth] in the early American era — but I’m not sure there’s been anything truly head-turning. Perhaps only once we land!

---

One sad note: Professor Perry Mehrling, who ran a [fantastic lecture series on money and banking](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KNEouYM5wRE&list=PLSuwqsAnJMtwZEwkJgHZCod2xP9b7skF5&index=2) that I have a great affection for, apparently [passed away](https://www.ineteconomics.org/perspectives/blog/remembering-perry-mehrling) a few weeks ago following an accidental fall.

Another sad note: now that British Columbia has done away with daylight savings time, California is _no longer on the same time_. Quite a hassle to coordinate with Vancouverites now! Though, given the Democratic Congress, perhaps it won’t be too long before we follow...

[^city]: Apologies to all the cities I haven’t been to that didn’t make the list. I’m sure Atlanta is lovely, but is it _must-see_?
[^cowen]: Complicated feelings on that one, for sure. He has such a distinct viewpoint that it’s fascinating to read, even if I don’t agree with 90% of what he says — but, at some point, I simply got too annoyed with his very let’s say _speculative_ attitude.
[^growth]: Apparently New Orleans was the _third largest_ city in the United States by the time of the Civil War, and by far the dominant financial and cultural capital of the South. That makes sense, of course — but for some reason I never thought of New Orleans as _that_ important.
